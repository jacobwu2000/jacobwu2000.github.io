## Ancient Testimonia

[PLACEHOLDER: literary references to Trikka's reputed status as an early center of the Asklepios cult.]

<PersonalObservations>
The city of Trikala was home to the ancient city-state of Trikka, home to the oldest sanctuary to Asclepius. It was one of the earliest locations of the cult of Asclepius as a healer-hero, and certain traditions place it as the birthplace of the god. Today, there are barely any ruins of this once-prominent Asclepieion, save for a small, closed-off archaeological site that I visited. Near Trikala is the city of Meteora, which is the home of several Eastern Orthodox monasteries built atop towering sandstone megaliths. The view during the sunset was truly breathtaking.

In the Iliad's Catalogue of Ships, Homer mentions 30 ships from Trikka, led by the healer-hero sons of Asclepius, Machaon and Podalirius. These brothers famously healed many of the Greek soldiers including Menelaus and Philoctetes. One account holds that they surgically removed Philoctetes' wound from a snakebite that had festered so much the Greeks abandoned him.
