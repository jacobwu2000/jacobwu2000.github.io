<PersonalObservations>
Epidaurus was home to the single most important Asclepieion in the Ancient Greek world. First built around the 4th century BC, it was a widely-visited panhellenic site. When I visited Epidaurus, I saw everything from the incredibly well-preserved theater on Mt. Cynortion to the ruins of the sanctuary itself, including the Catagogion, the ritual baths, the stadium for the *Asclepiea* games, the Hestiatorion dining hall (sometimes mistakenly called the gymnasium), the temple itself, the *tholos* tomb, and of course, the Abaton.

<PhotoPlaceholder caption="[PLACEHOLDER: the theater on Mt. Cynortion]" />

The Abaton, coming from Greek ἀ- ("not") and βαίνω ("walk"), means something like a place that cannot be walked on (similar to the Adyton at Delphi). It was the hall where a practice known as *enkoimesis*, or incubation, would take place. There, an ailing patient would lie down and sleep, and in their dreams they would be visited by some form of the god Asclepius, or perhaps his daughter Hygeia. When they woke up, they would be cured, or have received the instructions for treatment.
